## Who I am as an editor

I tell stories visually. My work is **intentional, immersive and suspenseful**:
- every element is there on purpose
- the viewer is pulled inside the world instead of watching it from outside
- the story always holds something back

I'd rather master framing, timing and sound than pile on effects. My tone is more serious than most editors', and I let images carry what other editors say with words and captions. [Q5, Q6]

## Principles

1. **Let it breathe.** I believe stillness is what gives motion its meaning, so I hold shots and graphics longer than feels safe, which is why I never fill every second with something happening. [Q5, Q6]
2. **Show it, don't caption it.** I believe the picture should tell the story before the words do, so I build meaning through framing and the order of images, which is why I never rely on flashy captions to make a video engaging. [Q5, Q6]
3. **Everything earns its place.** I believe every element is a decision, so anything that doesn't serve the story or the feeling gets cut, which is why I never add a graphic, transition or sound just because there's room for one. [Q5]
4. **Withhold, then reveal.** I believe suspense comes from making the viewer wait for something they want, so the answer arrives a beat after the question, which is why I never show the payoff at the start of a moment. [Q5]
5. **Graphics live inside the world.** I believe a graphic that looks like a sticker on the screen breaks immersion, so graphics share the footage's grain, light and camera movement, which is why I never drop a clean, flat overlay onto cinematic footage. [Q5]
6. **Make big things feel big.** I believe the strongest feeling is being small in front of something vast, so I show scale through distance, slowness and space, which is why I never shrink something huge into a quick number or a small icon. [Q7]
7. **The feeling comes first.** I believe music tells me how a moment should feel, so I choose the emotional register before I cut, which is why I never pick music last just to fill silence. [Q7, a guess about your process]
8. **Marketing, told cinematically.** I believe a business story deserves the same craft as a film, so client work gets real story structure, which is why I never make an ad that only feels like an ad. [Q5]

## Storytelling rules

- **Visual first:** if a shot can say it, the voiceover doesn't. [Q5]
- **Layered:** small details set up early come back later carrying meaning, like the connections you love finding in the Bible. Prefer recurring visual symbols over explanation. [Q7]
- **Serious, not hyped:** the tone sits closer to film than to "content". [Q5]
- **Basics over effects:** simple framing done perfectly beats a complex effect. [Q6]

## Motion graphics direction: the gap you could own

Most motion graphics on social media are built for hype: constant movement, fast pops, high-energy transitions. That includes the creators most people learn from. Your principles point the opposite way, and almost nobody works like that: **motion graphics that build suspense and awe instead of hype.** Fewer graphics, held longer, revealed more slowly, and living inside the footage.

**Example: the "0 to 20 million" moment** [Q3]

- **What the AI made:** a line climbs to 20M, a counter ticks up, and a whoosh plays.
- **Through these principles** (one possible version; you decide):
  - The camera stays tight on the tip of the line as it climbs, so you can't see how high it's going.
  - The years pass quietly.
  - The music thins out.
  - A held beat of near-silence.
  - Then the camera pulls back, and keeps pulling back, until the starting point is a speck.
  - The number lands only after the reveal, on a single sound hit, and then everything holds still.

Both use the same data. The difference is entirely in the decisions: withholding, scale, silence and stillness.

## Never-list

- Something happening every second just to hold attention [Q5, Q6]
- Captions doing the work the visuals should do [Q6]
- High-energy transitions used instead of storytelling [Q6]
- Graphics that just repeat the words (a number is said, so a graph of that number goes up) [Q3]
- Guessing what "punchy" or "smooth" means. Those words map to calibrated numbers, never guesses [Q3]
- Rough, uneven zooms and transitions [Q3]

# Think-aloud protocol

Most of your editing knowledge works below the level of words. You feel that a cut is late before you know why. Talking out loud while you work catches those reasons as they happen, which is how apprentices learn from a master.

## Three kinds of session

**1. Live narration** (20–40 min). Edit something real and talk the whole time.

**2. Commentary** (10–20 min). Play one of your finished edits and talk over it like a director's commentary track. Pause wherever there's a decision worth explaining.

**3. The deliberate default.** This is the most revealing exercise.
- Take one 15–30 second section and edit it twice.
- First, edit it the obvious way, fast, like an average editor or an AI would.
- Then edit it your way.
- Export both, and narrate the differences.

The gap between the two versions is your style in its purest form.

## Setup

- Screen recording with your mic on. On your Mac, press **Cmd + Shift + 5**, open **Options**, and choose your microphone.
- Keep the Resolve timeline and Inspector visible when you can.
- Raw and unedited is fine. Don't make it nice.

## What to say out loud

- What you're looking for when you scrub through footage
- Why you're trying something, and **why you undo it**
- Gut reactions before you can explain them: "this feels late", "too busy", "doesn't breathe". These are the most valuable.
- Every time you nudge something by a frame or two, what you were correcting
- What you're listening for in the music or VO
- When you decide something is done, and how you know

## What not to do

- Don't clean up your language or explain basics.
- Don't perform. Mumbling, swearing and half-sentences are all fine.

## After each session

Send the recording, or just the audio plus a transcript; Premiere, Resolve and Descript can all generate one. I'll pull out candidate rules and send them back as a list. You mark each one ✅ keep, ❌ wrong, or ✏️ fix the wording. The kept ones go into the style bible.

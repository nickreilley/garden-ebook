Chapter One

Weed Work

Identifying and uprooting what's choking your potential

Let me share something that changed everything for me.

"Above ALL else, guard your heart, for everything you do flows from it."

— Proverbs 4:23

The person who said this? Solomon—widely regarded as the wisest human to ever live. Even God Himself endorsed Solomon's wisdom (and that's about as high an endorsement as anyone can hope to get, right?).

Think about that for a second. This incredibly wise king, after accumulating all the knowledge and experience a person could possibly gather, says this one thing matters more than anything else: guarding your heart.

Not "work harder." Not "network more." Not "find your passion." But guard. your. heart.

Why? Because your heart—your inner garden—is the source of everything you do. Every decision. Every relationship. Every achievement. Every failure. It all flows from what's growing inside you.

❧

Your Attention Is Water

Here's something I want you to really get: your attention is like water in a garden.

Whatever you water grows. It's that simple.

When you give your attention to a thought, a feeling, or an idea—whether toxic or beneficial—you're watering it. You're helping it grow stronger roots in your garden.

This is why every business, app, and brand is fighting for your attention. They know that attention equals water for the seeds they're trying to plant in your mind. They want your water to grow what they've planted.

And they're not subtle about it. The average person now sees between 4,000 to 10,000 ads every single day. Each one is saying, "Water me! Water me!"

But here's the thing—you only have so much water to give. You can't water everything. So you've got to be selfish (in the best possible way) about what you choose to water and give your attention to.

❧

The Security Paradox

Here's something crazy when you think about it:

We obsess over cybersecurity. We create complicated passwords. We pay for premium security software. We freak out if our credit card number might be compromised.

Some of us even deleted TikTok when we heard it might be sharing our data!

But let me ask you this: When was the last time you invested that same level of protection into your heart?

Be honest. Have you ever gone to great lengths—to the point where you're willing to pay hundreds or even thousands of dollars—to guard what's truly important?

The very source of meaning and purpose in your life barely gets deliberate attention, while superficial information gets your stress, anxiety, and dollars. Meanwhile, your heart—the place where your authentic purpose, unshakable happiness, and genuine drive comes from—gets choked away silently by weeds you didn't even know were taking root.

Why Nothing Has Worked So Far

Let me guess: You've read the self-help books. You've watched the motivational TikToks. You've subscribed to YouTube channels promising to help you have your "best year ever." Maybe you've even paid for courses or tried different techniques.

And it's not that those things don't work...it's that they never last.

Eventually, they all fall into the same bin: the "tried it and meh, nothing's changed" bin.

Here's why: your garden isn't in a position to allow new seeds to grow. The soil isn't optimal, and the weeds are refusing to allow anything new to take root. This means that no matter how many good habits, routines, or ideas you try to plant in your mind and heart, they never fully develop—and you always end up back at square one, feeling even more frustrated than before.

First Things First: Weed Before You Plant

Before we can plant anything new, we first need to tend to the weeds in our garden. And that starts with being able to identify what's actually growing in there.

Meditation – The Misunderstood Weed-Finder

What if everything you know about meditation is making it harder, not easier? If you've ever tried meditating and thought, "Am I even doing this right?"—you're not alone.

Most people, even the ones teaching meditation, are probably teaching it in a way that makes it nearly impossible to succeed.

You sit down, close your eyes, try to "clear your mind"... but instead, your thoughts get louder. "Why is this so hard?" "Am I doing this wrong?" "I don't have time for this."

Here's the truth: You're not failing at meditation. You've just been taught the wrong idea of what it actually is.

The biggest misconception about meditation is that it's about emptying your mind or detaching from thoughts. That's completely backward. Meditation is actually the opposite. It's about immersion.

Your thoughts aren't distractions. They're the key to understanding what's growing in your garden.

❧

Let's Try Something Different: The IMMERSE Method™

Let's break down a simpler way to approach meditation as a weed-finding tool:

1

Identify Why We Meditate

The most valuable thing you have in life is your attention. And everything and everyone wants a piece of it.

Your past regrets? They want your attention. Your team's expectations? They want your attention. Your relationships? Want your attention. Your fears, anxieties, to-do lists? All screaming for your attention.

But what's the one voice you can barely hear through all this noise? Your own.

That quiet, precise guidance inside you—the one that says: "You're enough." "You can do this." "Go left instead of right."

Top performers consistently report that their biggest breakthroughs came from trusting their intuition. But to trust yourself, you have to hear yourself first. And that's why we meditate.

2

Map Your Thoughts (Weed Identification)

Let's try something right now. I want you to close your eyes for just 60 seconds.

Once they're closed, don't try to clear your mind. Instead, when a thought comes, simply label it:

  • "Why am I even doing this?" → Skepticism
  • "I should be working..." → Pressure
  • "Did I lock the door?" → Distraction

Now—open your eyes.

What just happened? You became an observer instead of just a participant in your thoughts.

Notice how I didn't ask you to empty your mind? That's because most meditation techniques fail by fighting against your brain's natural function.

This is why meditation feels frustrating at first. Your mind is used to getting carried away by every thought, so when you try to sit still, it fights back.

But the trick isn't to resist the thoughts—it's to dive in. This is how we can identify seeds that have taken root in our mind.

Meditation and the refusal to judge or be driven by thoughts—but simply label them—is how we give attention to our own garden and observe what's actually growing there. We don't want to just destroy everything. We want to take time to truly observe, searching for the weeds and toxic elements that impede growth in our garden.

Think of yourself as a gardener walking through your garden with a notepad, simply taking inventory before making any changes. "Ah, there's some anxiety growing over here... and look, some perfectionism over there... oh, and there's some imposter syndrome creeping in around the edges."

This weeding process isn't a one-and-done thing. Remember, the garden is something you actively guard and protect, and that means taking inventory on a regular basis of what might be deciding to take root in your soil. Meditate and label the thoughts that come—the more you do this, the better and clearer what is taking root will become.

A Reward for Your Journey So Far

Hey, you've stuck with me this far, which tells me you're serious about transforming your garden. So I want to give you a bonus—another incredibly powerful way to use meditation in your day-to-day life. Consider this your reward for investing in yourself already.

3

Master Real-World Application

Most people rush to solve things. They stress, overthink, and panic.

But the secret to better decisions? Spend more time understanding the problem than rushing to fix it.

Albert Einstein once said that if he had an hour to solve a problem, he would spend 55 minutes thinking about the problem and 5 minutes working to solve it.

Meditation trains your mind to do this automatically. Instead of reacting emotionally, you analyze:

  • What am I really feeling?
  • What's the root issue?
  • What's actually in my control?
4

Environment Hacking

Most people think meditation requires a silent retreat in the mountains. That's not real life.

What if I told you the noisier your environment, the more powerful your meditation can become?

Here's the hack: use technology to force immersion, even during your busiest days.

  • Noise-canceling earbuds (even the small, discreet ones work)
  • A focused breathing playlist
  • Five minutes between meetings—even in your car or a quiet corner of the office

MIT neuroscience research shows that even a 5-minute session reduces activity in your brain's stress center by up to 15%.

5

Reconnect with Scripture (For Those Who Want To)

If faith guides your life, here's where this becomes even more powerful.

We often see the very Son of God, perfect in all ways, remove himself from everyone during the day and night to pray. Why? Wasn't he already praying and fasting enough?

The answer connects directly to what we've discussed: to stay out of your head and not operate from emotion but remain clear-minded, patient, and in complete control when making decisions.

For Christians, this meditation time becomes holy ground. Simply pray while thinking of James 1:5 (asking for wisdom) or Philippians 4:7. Lift up an upcoming meeting, a difficult coworker, or focus for a challenging task.

And as Scripture promises, "the peace of God that surpasses all understanding will guard your hearts and minds."

Jesus dealt with the same human limitations we face—people with competing needs and motives. To ensure emotions didn't control Him, He would meditate and pray even in the midst of His busiest days.

And for those of you that believe in God, the saying is this: Meet God before you meet the devil. Do not venture out into the world or reach for your phone before you have met with and spent time with the Lord.

Remember Jesus' instruction to "pray that you do not fall into temptation"—and please don't cheapen temptation to mean only doing naughty things or dropping f-bombs. In the Bible, sin is defined as "missing the mark"—falling short of the target. So pray that you do not fall into the temptation of mediocrity.

Your Father is God, King of the universe. The expectations and power placed into His children—you and me—are incalculable. This is why it is absolutely vital you meet God before you meet the devil in the world. It will help you remain in a state of excellence in all that you do.

Try it. You'll be amazed at how much more growth happens with this focused watering than with hours of scattered misting across your entire garden.

Content Over Packaging: Choosing Seeds Wisely

When deciding what seeds are important, we often make a mistake: we value the packaging over the content.

Think about it this way—if your best friend, meaning well, unknowingly offers you poison, you shouldn't drink it just because it came from someone you love. By that same token, if your greatest enemy offers you a nutritious gift by accident or guilt, it would be wise to accept it.

It's not the person or the package from which the seed comes—it's the content of the seed we should concern ourselves with. This is why you should regard all people as equal and not judge nor hate any person. That very hate is a seed of the sneaky variety. It's a poison not planted by an outsider but by the gardener themselves.

"Anger is like drinking poison and expecting someone else to die."

We dare not waste such a limited resource like the water of our attention to nurture seeds of judgment, anger, or hatred.

The best gift we can give to the world is untainted fruit coming from the true depths of who we are. But let's not forget—just because now we know better doesn't mean we should look down on our friends and family who don't understand these things yet. This is about you, not others.

We must do what's best for the garden of your heart. But at the end of the day, a garden grows things exponentially. So if the seed you've planted is love, know this: the fruit is not only for you. The world around you will sample the things coming from your heart. This means everything you put your hands to, everything you say, the way you listen... everything will be encapsulated in this love, and it will be felt and experienced by your world.

Chapter Three

Growing Conditions

Creating the optimal environment for growth and harvesting your garden's fruit

Welcome to Chapter 3! If you've made it this far, you've already done some serious gardening work. You've identified the weeds that need to be pulled, and you've carefully selected authentic seeds that resonate with who you truly are.

Now comes a part that many gardeners overlook—creating the right conditions for those seeds to thrive.

The Environment Matters More Than You Think

I used to think growth was simple: plant good seeds, water them consistently, and boom—guaranteed results.

But here's what I learned the hard way: you can have the highest quality seeds and the most diligent watering schedule, but if your environment isn't conducive to growth, those seeds will struggle.

Think about it—have you ever tried to grow something in poor soil? Or in a spot that gets too little sunlight? Or where there's no protection from harsh elements?

The same principles apply to your heart garden. Your environment—the places you go, the people you surround yourself with, the information you consume, the routines you follow—all create the conditions that either support or sabotage your growth.

❧

The Four Elements of Optimal Growing Conditions

Just like plants need certain elements to thrive, the seeds in your heart garden need specific conditions to flourish. Let's break them down:

1. The Soil: Where It All Begins

In Matthew 13:3-8, Jesus tells a story about a farmer who went out to plant seed in his field. In this parable, we find some constants and some variables worth noting.

The constants in the story: the farmer—the person doing the planting is the same; and the seed—the type of seed being sown is the same across the board.

But the results? Dramatically different.

🐦

Some seed fell by a path, but birds came and ate it

🪨

Some fell on rocky places where the soil was shallow, so the plants sprang up quickly, but when the sun came up, they withered because they had no root

🌿

Some seeds fell among thorns, where the thorns grew up and choked the plants

🌾

And lastly, some seed fell on good soil, where it produced 100, 60, or 30 times what was sown

This parable provides an amazing guide to what you need to consider before investing anything of value. I want you to understand something crucial: effort and hard work aren't what matters most—it's effort and hard work on the right thing placed under optimal conditions for growth.

Direction, direction, direction, direction is superior to speed. Only accelerate when you've 100% determined the direction.

This time, we're consciously choosing to water something different.

2. The Support System: Optimizing Your Environment

You're going to laugh at me for this, but I can't help it—this is just how I am. While writing the 2nd tip for growing conditions, it's actually supposed to be about water, but I love giving you the best parts as soon as possible. Since we've already touched on attention and water a little, I rewrote this just so you could have this tidbit now.

You should finish this whole chapter, of course, but even if you bail on it now, know you're getting the meaty stuff up front.

There are a lot of different things needed to optimize and further enhance the conditions a plant grows under. All these little things are what I would call the support system. Everything from manure to pruners. These are things that further enhance the conditions under which a plant grows.

Here's the thing, and it relates to our previous talk about poison even from a friend: You have to become vigilant about the people you allow around you when it's in your control. And I don't mean just people in your family, friend, or work groups. No, I mean even people you choose to listen to through media and its various forms.

It takes deliberate effort to nurture and grow what lies inside you. Every person has this amazing gift for themselves and the world just chilling dormant inside. But here's the issue—why are you expecting people that don't prioritize their own heart to prioritize yours?

It's not someone else's job to fulfill you and give you purpose or meaning. No, but it is that purpose and meaning you're meant to share with and celebrate with others. You need to vet the things being said to you and the environments you're being asked to be a part of. When it is in your control, place your heart in conditions optimal for it, not detrimental to it.

When you hop onto TikTok or YouTube, you'll notice that if you watch a lot of bullfighting videos, then you'll start getting recommended and presented a lot of content that's about bullfighting, or subjects related to bullfighting, maybe even travel guide videos to Spain because it appears to be the bullfighting capital.

See, even industry media giants like ByteDance and Google realize the power of immersing their users with more of what they're asking for. So much value in it that companies pay uncomfortable amounts for the chance to catch your attention while you're immersed in this world custom-made for what you like.

Here's a trick we can use to take advantage of this and further optimize the quality of our environment: Make a new email address and create a new TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, etc. But this time, teach the algorithm to only present you with things and content relevant to nurturing what's in your heart and where you're trying to go.

If, for example, you're passionate about charisma and this is something you're watering—the ability to capture and hold the room and people's attention through speech and body language—then for your entertainment, you might choose to watch TV shows like "Suits," immersing yourself with characters like Harvey Specter who shine when it comes to charisma.

What's my point? Make everything around you serve the seed you're watering. Your entertainment, your education, your friendships. You can't control everything—this is why it is so vital you optimize what you can control. Make every aspect of the environment you can control serve you.

3. The Water: Your Attention and Focus

We've already talked quite a bit about how your attention is like water—whatever you focus on grows. But there's another aspect to this: the quality of your attention matters just as much as where you direct it.

Many of us are watering a hundred different plants with tiny droplets, wondering why nothing is flourishing. We're physically present in one place while mentally sprinkling our attention across a dozen different thoughts, worries, or distractions. This is like trying to grow plants with occasional misting—they might survive, but they'll never truly thrive.

The seeds in your heart garden need deep, consistent watering to develop strong roots and produce the kind of fruit we talked about earlier—that 30x to 100x return.

Here's how to improve the quality of your watering:

  • Deep watering over shallow sprinkling: Focus intensely on one thing rather than spreading your attention thin
  • Create irrigation systems: Set up environments that naturally direct your attention where it needs to go. If you want to dive deeper into this concept, I highly recommend the book "Thinking in Systems" by Donella Meadows. She brilliantly explains how systems shape our behavior more than willpower ever could.
  • Clear blockages: Identify and remove distractions that divert your water to weeds
  • Schedule watering times: Dedicate specific blocks for focused attention on what matters most
  • Monitor the flow: Learn to recognize when your attention is being diverted and gently redirect it

One watering technique that's been game-changing for me: I set a timer for just 25 minutes and commit to watering ONE area of my garden during that time. No checking my phone, no answering emails, no jumping to other plants. Just 25 minutes of deep watering in one specific spot. When the timer goes off, I take a 5-minute break, then either continue or move to another part of the garden.

Let me share a personal practice that's transformed my own garden. I've made it a point to never get on social media of any kind before 12pm. This is especially powerful on days when I have no appointments or commitments until after noon. Why? Because from the time I wake up till noon, the only thing I'm watering and giving attention to is what's in my heart. Before meeting the strangers of the world, I choose to meet with my own heart first.

This small change has produced incredible results. Those morning hours—when my mind is clearest, my willpower is strongest, and the world hasn't started making demands—become sacred time for tending what truly matters. By the time I do engage with the outside world, I'm operating from a place of connection with my authentic self rather than reacting to whatever happens to be trending that day.

And for those of you who believe in God, the saying is this: Meet God before you meet the devil. Do not venture out into the world or reach for your phone before you have met with and spent time with the Lord.

Remember Jesus' instruction to "pray that you do not fall into temptation"—and please don't cheapen temptation to mean only doing naughty things or dropping f-bombs. In the Bible, sin is defined as "missing the mark"—falling short of the target. So pray that you do not fall into the temptation of mediocrity.

Your Father is God, King of the universe. The expectations and power placed into His children—you and me—are incalculable. This is why it is absolutely vital you meet God before you meet the devil in the world. It will help you remain in a state of excellence in all that you do.

Try it. You'll be amazed at how much more growth happens with this focused watering than with hours of scattered misting across your entire garden.

4. The Protection: Guarding Your Heart's Gate

Remember what we discussed earlier? The mind is not the heart—it's the door, the filter, the gatekeeper to what enters your garden. This is why protection is so crucial.

In Proverbs 4:23, Solomon didn't casually suggest we should "pay attention" to our hearts. He said to guard it above all else—with vigilance, intentionality, and fierce protection. Why? Because everything you do flows from it.

Think about your mind as the fence surrounding your garden. Every thought, image, conversation, and experience must pass through this fence before it can take root in your heart. The question is: How strong and intentional is your fencing?

Many of us have fences full of holes. We allow any and every thought, worry, fear, or influence to stroll right into our garden, never questioning whether they belong there. Others have built rigid, impenetrable walls that keep out both harmful and helpful influences. Neither approach creates optimal growing conditions.

Healthy boundaries look like a well-maintained fence with gates that YOU control:

  • Conscious consumption: Being deliberate about what media, conversations, and environments you expose yourself to
  • Thought filtering: Learning to recognize and redirect thoughts that don't serve your garden
  • Strategic ignorance: Deliberately choosing not to know certain things that would only create mental clutter
  • Sacred spaces: Creating physical and temporal boundaries where your garden is especially protected
  • Clear communication: Letting others know how and when they can access your attention

One of the most powerful boundaries I've implemented is what I call the "mental doorman." Just as an exclusive building has someone at the door deciding who enters and who doesn't, I've trained myself to pause before allowing a thought to take residence in my mind.

When a thought knocks—especially a worrying, fearful, or negative one—I ask: "Does this thought serve my garden? Will allowing it in lead to fruit I want to harvest? Is it aligned with truth?"

If the answer is no, I don't try to force it away (which usually makes it knock louder). Instead, I acknowledge it: "I see you, but you don't get to come in right now." Then I redirect my attention to what I've intentionally chosen to grow.

But here's another crucial layer: your mental doorman doesn't just control what enters—it also guards what leaves. When someone is speaking to me or I'm having a conversation, I've become a lot slower in speaking and responding. I am truly trying to listen and think about what I'll say instead of just reacting. I want to be present and act as a doorman not only to what enters but also to what leaves my garden.

Jesus said, "Out of the abundance of the heart, the mouth speaks." He also taught that it's not what goes into a person that taints them—it's what comes out, because what comes out reveals the condition of their heart. This isn't just about words but actions too.

Yes, even those comments you leave online using burner accounts or even your main account. Those hurtful things you've said or done—they came from somewhere. We aren't here to run from what's inside. We're here to take ownership and account for what's there, and to begin planting what needs to be there instead.

No make-believe ideas here. I did and continue to do this work, and so do and will we all. The journey of tending your heart is lifelong, but each day of intentional care moves you closer to a garden that produces beautiful fruit.

Remember Jesus' words: "Don't throw your pearls to swine." This means that giving gold to something or someone that doesn't grasp its value isn't a fault on their part—it's on you. Value your own thoughts and opinions enough to not just throw them around for free. Invest your own thoughts and words with care. Vet also what leaves your garden.

Your mind is the gatekeeper of your heart. When you strengthen and intentionally operate this gate, you don't have to worry as much about weeding—because you're preventing weeds from entering in the first place.

Assessing Your Current Growing Conditions

Now that we've covered the four elements of optimal growing conditions, let's take a moment to honestly assess your current environment:

🌱 Soil Assessment

How's the condition of your heart's soil? Are you planting seeds in the good soil that yields 30, 60, or 100 times what was sown, or are you unknowingly dropping precious seeds among thorns, on rocky ground, or by the wayside?

🤝 Support System Assessment

What's surrounding and supporting the growth in your garden? Are your environments—both physical and digital—serving the seeds you want to grow? Are you intentionally curating influences that nurture rather than hinder your growth?

💧 Water Assessment

Where is your attention flowing? Are you deeply watering a few important plants, or are you misting dozens of plants with scattered attention? Have you established protected morning hours to water what matters most before engaging with the world?

🛡️ Protection Assessment

How well is your mind functioning as the gatekeeper of your heart? Are you actively filtering what enters, or is your fence full of holes? Have you established a "mental doorman" that evaluates thoughts before allowing them residence?

The purpose of this assessment isn't to make you feel bad about where you are. It's to help you identify specific areas where small, strategic adjustments could dramatically improve your growing conditions. Remember, this isn't about perfection—it's about direction and consistent improvement.

Write down one specific improvement you could make in each area. Then, instead of trying to change everything at once (which rarely works), choose the ONE adjustment that would have the greatest positive impact on your garden right now, and commit to implementing it this week.
